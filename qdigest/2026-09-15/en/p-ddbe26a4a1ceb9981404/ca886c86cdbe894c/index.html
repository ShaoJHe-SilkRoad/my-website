Engineered particles from young plasma helped Alzheimer-model mice.

Researchers collected tiny biological particles called exosomes from the plasma of young individuals. They attached a targeting peptide, RVG-29, to the surface to create an engineered version, RVG-EXOs, and compared it with the unmodified young-plasma particles after giving them to 3×Tg mice, a laboratory model of Alzheimer's disease.

In these Alzheimer-model mice, the engineered version could more efficiently enter brain tissue and target neurons. It also could significantly reduce Aβ plaque and phosphorylated Tau (P-Tau) pathological deposition, restore synaptic structure, promote neuronal survival, and improve cognitive behavior.

The findings are limited to Alzheimer-model mice. The authors describe the work as revealing potential value for Alzheimer's treatment and providing new insights, not as evidence that the approach would help people; whether it would be effective or safe in people is unknown.

Terms explained

exosomes

Tiny bag-like structures formed inside a cell and released by cells into the blood; they contain some of the cell's proteins, DNA, and RNA.

The tiny particles collected from young individuals' plasma and tested in Alzheimer's-model mice.
