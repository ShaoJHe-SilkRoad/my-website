Behind the project+
What it is for
Seeing how much Codex and Claude time is left before starting a long piece of work, without opening each account.
The hardest parts
Exact Claude usage comes from a signed-in Safari session, so AUV has to keep reading it when Safari is hidden, minimized or on another desktop. The other half of the work is shipping a signed, notarized Universal Mac app alongside a separate Windows version.
Where help is needed
Windows is the gap: it is still at 0.16.3, ships unsigned, and lags the Mac version. A developer who knows Windows packaging and code signing would make the biggest difference. Testing and design feedback from people who use Codex or Claude every day is just as welcome.

QDigest

Research, explained

Make room for understanding.

Source-grounded health research, explained in English and Simplified Chinese: concise email briefs with fuller explanations on the web.

Every explanation cites its original paper (journal, DOI, PubMed ID) and says when it relies on the abstract alone. Educational reading, not medical advice.

  • English + 中文
  • Source-conscious
  • Mobile-friendly

Behind the project+
The question
How can health research become easier to understand while keeping its evidence and uncertainty visible?
What I’m working toward
Getting new papers from an automated draft to an explanation I can trust, without rescuing each one by hand, so editions can come out on a regular schedule.
Where you can help
I’d like to hear from curious readers, editorial thinkers, and people who care about how information is presented.
QDigest · How it has changed

What changed, and what each change cost.

Decisions from September to October 2026

  1. Early Sep

    From summaries to a bilingual reader

    Research summaries became web pages in English and Simplified Chinese, with an email brief for each edition.

    Trade-offEvery article is now written and checked twice, once per language.

  2. 14 Sep

    First edition: five topics, fifteen articles

    The articles were accepted one by one.

    Trade-offCorrect articles did not add up to a good read. Fifteen pieces across five topics was too much to take in.

  3. 16 Sep

    Three topics, nine articles

    Alzheimer’s disease, stroke, and cancer prevention and research.

    Trade-offFewer subjects, so each article can go deeper. Earlier editions stay online.

  4. 16–17 Sep

    Two depths: Lazy and Full

    Lazy puts a short, plain takeaway for every article on one page. Full explains each paper in depth, explains its terms, and leads on to the next article. Going back returns you to where you were.

    Trade-offTwo texts per paper to keep in step. I dropped the one-sentence rule for Lazy: a short paragraph that reads naturally beats a sentence squeezed to fit.

  5. Sep–Oct

    Automating new papers

    Model-written drafts led with statistics, got facts wrong, and slipped past automated review. I now record what the model wrote first, what it corrected, and what a person changed.

    Trade-offSlower, and no new edition since 16 September. I won’t hide a weak pipeline by rewriting its drafts myself.

  6. Oct

    Reading settings and Reading Lab

    Saved reading preferences, more English and Chinese fonts (including OpenDyslexic and Traditional Chinese), and a Reading Lab for comparing settings on fictional samples. Focus mode is hidden for now.

    Trade-offBuilt, but it ships with the next edition rather than being bolted onto the old one.

ScreenSnap

Screenshot lens

One click. Whole screen. On your clipboard.

A small floating lens that copies a screenshot of every monitor to your clipboard. Click it or press Alt+C in any app, then paste.

  • Windows 10 / 11
  • Alt+C shortcut
  • Nothing to install
Get ScreenSnap↗
Behind the project+
What it is for
Getting what is on your screen into a message or document in one motion, without saving files.
How to start
Download the ZIP from the ScreenSnap page, unblock it, extract it and open ScreenSnap.vbs. The lens appears near the top-right of your screen.
Good to know
It uses the built-in Windows PowerShell, never saves or sends your screenshots, and hides itself so it is never in the picture.
